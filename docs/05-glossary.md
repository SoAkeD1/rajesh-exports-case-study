# 5. Glossary

Every term in the memo that is not everyday English, explained without assuming
any finance background. Terms are grouped by topic rather than alphabetically,
because they make more sense in context.

---

## Money and company basics

**Revenue** — the total money coming in from sales, before any costs are taken
out. Also called turnover or "the top line". A shop that sells ₹100 of goods has
₹100 of revenue, even if the goods cost it ₹99 to buy.

**Profit (PAT)** — what is actually left after every cost and tax has been paid.
PAT stands for **Profit After Tax**. This is the number that belongs to the
owners. Also called "the bottom line".

**Margin** — profit expressed as a percentage of revenue. Sell for ₹100 and keep
₹0.02, and your margin is 0.02%. Low margins are normal in some businesses
(supermarkets, gold trading) and abnormal in others (software).

**Crore and lakh** — Indian number units. 1 lakh = 100,000. 1 crore = 10 million
(100 lakh). So ₹4,23,099 crore is roughly ₹4.23 trillion. **1 lakh crore** = 1
trillion.

**FY21, FY25** — financial years. In India a financial year runs April to March,
so FY25 means April 2024 to March 2025.

**Book value** — what the company's accounts say it is worth: everything it owns
minus everything it owes.

## Company structures

**Subsidiary** — a company owned by another company. The owner is the "parent".

**Step-down subsidiary** — a subsidiary of a subsidiary. Two levels down from the
parent.

**Holding company** — a company whose only job is to own other companies. It does
not manufacture or trade anything itself. This is why revenue appearing at a
holding company is odd: there is no underlying activity there to generate it.

**Standalone** — the parent company's own figures, by itself, ignoring everything
it owns.

**Consolidated** — the parent plus all its subsidiaries added together, as if
they were one business. In this case the difference is enormous: standalone
revenue is 1.66% of consolidated revenue.

**Related-party transaction** — a deal between companies that are connected to
each other, for example two subsidiaries of the same parent. Not automatically
improper, but it needs disclosure, because a group can move money between its
own pockets and make it look like trade.

**Circular transaction** — money or goods going around a loop and returning to
where they started, creating the appearance of sales without any real outside
customer. A classic way to inflate revenue.

## Auditing and regulation

**Audit** — an independent accounting firm checks a company's books and formally
states whether they give a true and fair picture.

**Audited vs unaudited** — audited figures have been checked and signed off by an
outsider. Unaudited figures are just what management prepared. The gap between
the two is central to this case.

**Statutory accounts** — the official, legally required, audited financial
statements an entity files in its own country.

**Qualified opinion** — when an auditor signs off *but adds a caveat*, saying
there is something they could not verify or do not agree with. A warning sign.

**Auditor resignation** — the auditor quits mid-engagement. Usually a serious
signal, because auditors rarely walk away from paying clients without reason.

**Restatement** — a company formally corrects figures it previously published.
It is an admission that the earlier numbers were wrong.

**Forensic audit** — a deeper, investigative examination that goes beyond a
normal audit, specifically looking for misstatement or fraud. It traces
individual transactions rather than sampling.

**Transaction-level records** — the underlying paperwork for individual deals:
invoices, delivery notes, bank transfers. As opposed to summary totals. The
regulator asked for these and says it did not get them.

**Interim order** — a temporary ruling issued while an investigation is still
ongoing. Not a final verdict, and it can be modified or withdrawn later.

**Prima facie** — Latin for "on first look". It means the evidence *appears* to
show something, at a preliminary glance. A low bar, deliberately.

**Adjudication** — the final, formal decision at the end of a legal or regulatory
process. This case has not reached that stage.

## Investing

**Long-only fund** — a fund that only buys shares hoping they go up. It cannot
profit from a share falling. So for a long-only fund, a bad holding is simply a
loss with no offsetting gain.

**AUM (Assets Under Management)** — the total money a fund manages on behalf of
its clients. Aarohan Capital's is ₹4,500 crore in the case.

**Fiduciary capital / fiduciary duty** — money managed on behalf of other people,
and the legal obligation to act in their interest rather than your own. This is
why "we cannot analyse it" is a sufficient reason to sell: you are not allowed to
take unmeasurable risks with someone else's money.

**Position** — the amount of a particular share a fund holds.

**Legacy position** — a holding bought a while ago, often under a thesis that no
longer applies.

**Average down** — buying more of a share after its price has fallen, to lower
your average purchase price. Sensible if the fall is irrational; a way to
multiply losses if it is not.

**Investable universe** — the list of companies a fund is willing to consider at
all. Removing a company from the universe is stronger than just selling it: it
means "we will not look at this again until something specific changes".

**Valuation screen** — an automated filter that ranks companies by ratios to find
cheap-looking ones. The trap in this case: a screen uses reported figures, so if
those figures are wrong, the screen's answer is meaningless.

**Price-to-sales (P/S)** — share price compared to revenue per share.

**Price-to-book (P/B)** — share price compared to book value per share. A low P/B
usually suggests a share is cheap — but only if the book value is real.

**De-rating** — when the market permanently decides a company deserves a lower
valuation multiple than before. Even if profits recover, the share does not
return to its old rating.

**Impairment** — a permanent loss of value, as opposed to a temporary dip.

**Lower circuit** — a daily limit on how far an Indian share price may fall
before trading halts. Relevant here because if bad news lands, the price hits
the circuit with no buyers, and you physically cannot sell. This is the core
reason the memo rejects "wait and see".

**Liquidity** — how easily you can sell something without moving its price. Exit
liquidity disappearing means there is nobody left to sell to.

**Optionality** — the value of being able to choose later. The memo argues that
here the optionality of waiting is *negative*: you gain no useful choice by
waiting, and you lose the ability to act.

## Gold and refining

**Bullion** — gold (or silver) in bulk form — bars and ingots — valued by weight
and purity rather than as jewellery.

**Refinery** — a facility that melts and purifies raw or scrap gold into standard
bars. Valcambi SA, in Switzerland, is one of the world's largest.

**Throughput** — the total volume of material passing through a facility. A
refinery with high throughput handles enormous value even though it earns only a
small fee on each gram.

**Value-addition** — the fee a refiner earns for its work, as distinct from the
value of the metal itself. A refiner might handle ₹1,000 of gold and earn ₹2.

**Gross vs net revenue** — the accounting choice at the centre of this case.
*Gross* means recording the full ₹1,000 of metal value as revenue. *Net* means
recording only the ₹2 fee. Both are legitimate in different circumstances, and
they differ by a factor of 500 here.

**Principal vs agent** — the accounting test that decides gross or net. If you
take ownership of the goods and carry the risk of the price moving, you are a
*principal* and record gross. If you merely arrange the deal for a fee, you are
an *agent* and record net.

**Chain of custody** — the documented trail showing who physically held an asset,
when, and where it moved. For gold this includes vault records and transport
documents.

**Assay certificate** — a document certifying the purity of a specific batch of
gold, issued after testing.

**Tonne** — 1,000 kilograms. World gold supply was 4,962 tonnes in 2024 and 5,002
tonnes in 2025, of which about 3,672 tonnes was newly mined (World Gold Council).

**Per-ounce fee** — how refiners are usually paid: a fixed charge for each ounce
handled, regardless of the gold price. So when the gold price rises, the refiner's
*revenue* can balloon while its *fee income* stays flat. This is why a rising
price makes margins look thinner without anything being wrong.

## Argument and reasoning terms

**Bull case** — the argument for why an investment will do well.

**Bear case** — the argument for why it will do badly.

**Steelman** — deliberately stating the *strongest possible* version of the
opposing argument before responding to it. The opposite of a strawman. The memo
steelmans the company's defence on purpose: refuting a weak version would prove
nothing.

**Falsifiable** — an claim specific enough that stated evidence could prove it
wrong. The memo's recommendation is falsifiable because it lists exactly what
would reverse it — which is what separates an analytical judgement from a hunch.

**Load-bearing** — the part of an argument that everything else rests on. Remove
it and the rest collapses. The gross-versus-net explanation is load-bearing for
the bull case.

**Circular reasoning** — using a conclusion as its own evidence. Here: "the stock
is cheap because the screen says so" — when the screen is fed by the very
figures under dispute.

**Denominator** — the bottom number in a fraction. The memo uses it as shorthand:
valuation ratios divide price by revenue, profit or book value, so if those
cannot be trusted there is no denominator and the ratio means nothing.

## The four options in this case

| Option | Plain English |
|---|---|
| **A — Buy / Average Down** | The market panicked. Buy more while it is cheap. |
| **B — Hold / Wait for Forensic Audit** | Serious concern, but do not sell until we know more. |
| **C — Exit / Avoid** | Sell what we hold and stay away. |
| **D — Forensic Red Flag / Uninvestable** | Sell, *and* strike the company off the list of things we will even consider, until independent verification arrives. |

This submission chose **D**.
