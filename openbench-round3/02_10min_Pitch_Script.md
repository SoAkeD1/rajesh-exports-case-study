### Slide 1 · Cover (Mahi, 15 s)

"Good morning. We are Team Inferno. India has costly lab instruments sitting idle, while startups can't get a single test done on time. That is the idle-instrument paradox. Our answer fits in four words: booked should mean delivered."

### Slide 2 · Abstract (Mahi, 50 s)

"Here is the whole answer first. OpenBench should sell delivered results, not listings. Three moves. One: a managed tier called Assured, where the customer gets a committed slot, a sealed sample and an on-time guarantee. That lifts on-time from 78% today to 92% by Month 12. Two: grow in a deliberate order, to 17 labs by Month 12, five more than the investor asks. Three: OpenBench breaks even in Month 15 and uses only ₹38.6 lakh of the ₹1.2 crore. On the investor, we renegotiate, not reject: the same money in two halves, ₹60 lakh now and ₹60 lakh after a Month-7 reliability check."

### Slide 3 · Why customers leave (Mahi, 65 s incl. flash)

"Only 42% of customers come back within 90 days. We trace this to three causes. First, supply is listed, not productised: only 38 of 86 instruments are truly bookable, labs cancel 14% of jobs and deliver 8% late. Second, labs earn too little per hassle, so when teaching clashes with an outside job, the outside job loses. Third, there is no trust layer: 31% of customers worry about turnaround, 27% about confidentiality. And it is not just OpenBench. India's free portal, I-STEM, carries out only about half of its requests.

Different customers leave for different reasons. This strip shows it, and the detail is in appendix A8."

"So a better listing won't fix this. A guaranteed outcome will."

### Slide 4 · Pilot economics (Mahi, 45 s)

"Now the money. On an ₹8,500 booking, OpenBench keeps ₹1,530 and spends ₹600, leaving ₹930. Seventy bookings a month gives ₹65,100 against ₹3.6 lakh of fixed cost, so the pilot loses about ₹2.95 lakh every month. To break even it needs 387 bookings a month, 5.5 times today. But pushing volume on a broken service just multiplies cancellations. So: reliability first, then scale. Minakshi will show which model does that."

### Slide 5 · Model choice (Minakshi, 65 s)

"We scored the three models in the case, plus a hybrid, on six weighted criteria. Reliability carries the most weight because both the investor's 90% target and the customer's return depend on it. The marketplace is cheap but stuck at 82% on-time. Managed fulfilment reaches 94% but is costly. Lab software fails on adoption: only 4 of 10 labs would pay. Our hybrid scores 82.

The customer pays per booking; we keep 28% on Assured, or 8% plus a lab subscription on Connect. We run everything around the instrument. Labs earn more, not less: a pilot lab's income rises 5.6%. And all tiers run on one backbone; only Assured runs until the Month-7 gate."

### Slide 6 · Market entry (Minakshi, 65 s incl. flash)

"Order matters. Month 1, we convert the four pilot labs to Assured. Then cluster A, the deep-tech belt: highest value, about ₹48,000 per lab a month, and its worry, IP, is exactly what our custody layer fixes. Then cluster C, the startup hub, same kind of customers. That is 13 labs in three cities by Month 8. The factory corridor, B, comes last, after the Month-7 gate, on a cheaper tier. We assume only 85% of the case's projected volume.

Most customers come free through incubators and I-STEM leads; the rest cost about ₹1,500 each. And finding 16 new labs is a funnel, in appendix A7."

### Slide 7 · Results (Minakshi, 80 s incl. flash)

"Every number here comes from one Excel model. The teal line is our base case: losses while we onboard, then break-even in Month 15, three months before the investor's deadline. 2,316 paid bookings in year one, and only ₹38.6 lakh used.

The red line is the downside: lower volume, lower prices, a two-month delay and cost overruns. It misses Month 18, but cash never drops below ₹41 lakh. If it happens, our Month-9 response cuts the loss further. All four scenarios are in A9."

→ **[Jump to 20 · A9, 10 s]** "Base, downside, upside, and downside with our rescue: loss narrows to ₹2.3 lakh, cash floor rises to ₹47.7 lakh." → **[back to 7]**

"And we are honest: break-even uses two levers, a priority rush option and cheaper fulfilment over time. Our +50% rush price is what a real accredited lab already charges. Switch both levers off and Month 18 is −₹1.23 lakh."

### Slide 8 · Investor terms (Minakshi, 60 s)

"We tested every investor condition against our own model. We beat the host target with 17 labs, beat 90% on-time, and break even before Month 18. Two asks we would miss honestly: 2,316 bookings against 2,400, and 58% repeat against 60%. The case gives only one data point linking reliability to repeat, and outside evidence puts it at 51 to 55%, as appendix A5 shows. So we propose 2,200 bookings and 55% repeat.

And the money comes in two halves: ₹60 lakh now, ₹60 lakh at Month 7 if we have ten labs live at 90% on-time. Even our downside only needs ₹37.6 lakh by then. The investor risks less and gets a stricter quality bar."

### Slide 9 · Trust layer (Swapnil, 50 s)

"This is what customers pay 28% for. Six steps. Labs commit staffed slots, and only those are bookable. Samples move under a sealed QR chain of custody. Calibration is checked before every test. The client owns the data under a three-party NDA. Payment sits in escrow and the lab is paid in seven days. Disputes go to mediation, then a neutral re-test. When something breaks, rules kick in: a cancelled job goes to a backup lab and the host loses 10% of its payout; a late result refunds 20%. On a listing portal, nobody is accountable. With Assured, we are."

### Slide 10 · Checkpoints (Swapnil, 30 s)

"The board will know early. Month 3: six labs live. Month 6: ten labs at 94% on-time, which sets up the Month-7 gate. Month 12: 17 labs, 2,316 bookings. Month 18: 20 labs and positive EBITDA. We watch five KPIs; the two that matter most are on-time, 78% to at least 90%, and cancellations, 14% to at most 5%."

### Slide 11 · Risks and the ask (Swapnil, 30 s)

"Every big risk has a number that warns us and an agreed reply. Fewer than ten labs at Month 7: we hold the second tranche. On-time under 88%: we stop opening cities. Demand under 70% at Month 9: we skip the cheapest tier and cut a city's cost. So we ask the board to approve the Assured-first plan and the two-tranche deal. Because booked should mean delivered. Thank you."
