## 1. Caveman basics: what is this?

One sentence: OpenBench should stop selling *listings* of lab machines and start selling *delivered results*, and take the investor's ₹1.2 crore in two halves.

**The competition**

- Tech Zephyr 4.0 Case Study Finale, IIT Bhubaneswar, Round 3, on 31 October 2026.
- We are **Team Inferno**: Mahi, Minakshi Singha, Swapnil Behera.
- Format: a PPT with at most **10 content slides** (title and appendix don't count). **10 minutes** to talk, then **5 minutes** of judge questions.
- Judges score: Problem (15), Feasibility (20), Strategy (20), Analysis (20), Presentation and Q&A (25) = 100.

**What is OpenBench? (the case company)**

- A website where labs (mostly colleges) list expensive machines that sit idle. Startups and companies book time on them to test samples.
- This is "The Idle Instrument Paradox": India has costly machines doing nothing, while small companies can't find a machine to use.
- OpenBench takes an **18% fee** on each booking. An average booking is **₹8,500**.

**The problem, caveman version**

- Machine is listed, but often not really bookable: only **38 of 86** listed machines can actually be booked.
- Labs cancel **14%** of jobs and deliver **8%** late.
- So only **42%** of customers come back within 90 days.
- OpenBench loses about **₹2.95 lakh every month**.

**Our answer, caveman version**

1. **Sell a promise, not a list.** New tier called *Assured*: booked slot is guaranteed, sample is sealed and tracked, result on time or 20% money back.
2. **Three price levels, one engine.** Assured (we keep 28%), Connect (lab pays ₹12k/month, we keep 8%), Verified (plain listing, 18%). Same backbone under all three.
3. **Grow in the right order.** Pilot city → deep-tech cluster A → startup hub C → price-sensitive factory belt B last.
4. **Money:** break even in **Month 15**. Take ₹1.2 crore as **₹60L now + ₹60L at Month 7**, only if quality targets are hit.
5. **Be honest with the investor:** we hit 3 of their 5 targets; for the other 2 we offer lower, realistic numbers plus a stricter quality promise.

## 4. The deck, slide by slide

The deck is dressed as a lab notebook: the top-right tracker reads Abstract → Observation → Hypothesis → Results → Method → Controls, like a science report. Every slide ends with a dashed **RESULT:** tag = the one thing to remember.

**Slide 1 · Cover — "The Idle Instrument Paradox"**

- Looks like a lab-report header: "Experiment 04", a CALIBRATED stamp, a lab-bench drawing.
- Tagline: **"Booked should mean delivered."** That line opens and closes the talk.

**Slide 2 · Abstract — the whole answer in one slide**

- Three boxes: *what OpenBench becomes* (92% on-time, Assured tier), *where it grows* (17 labs by M12), *what it earns* (break-even M15, uses ₹38.6L of ₹120L).
- Investor strip: ₹60L now → Month-7 gate → ₹60L. "Renegotiate, don't reject."
- Caveman: tell the judges the ending first.

**Slide 3 · Observation — why customers leave**

- 42% ring = only 42 of 100 customers come back.
- Three causes: (1) machines listed but not really available (44% bookable, 14% cancelled, 8% late); (2) labs earn too little for the hassle; (3) no trust layer (31% fear slow results, 27% fear data leaks).
- Dark box: India's free government portal I-STEM executes only ≈50% of requests, so the problem is the whole system, not just OpenBench.
- New "Who leaves" strip: deep-tech fears IP leaks, factories want price + predictable timing, seasonal users miss slots. Marked as inference. **Detail: A8.**

**Slide 4 · Observation — the pilot loses money**

- One booking: ₹1,530 fee − ₹600 support = ₹930.
- 70 bookings × ₹930 = ₹65,100, vs ₹3.6L fixed cost → loses ₹2.95L a month.
- Needs 387 bookings (5.5× today) to break even. Caveman: just pushing volume makes more broken promises; fix reliability first.

**Slide 5 · Hypothesis — which business model**

- Scoring table: Marketplace 56, Managed 77, Lab SaaS 48, **our hybrid 82**. Reliability has the biggest weight (25).
- Four cards: promise (booked = delivered), who pays (28% Assured, or 8% + ₹12k Connect), who operates (we run everything around the machine), why labs join (+5.6% income).
- New line: one backbone, three price points; only Assured runs until the Month-7 gate.

**Slide 6 · Hypothesis — where to grow, in what order**

- Map + Gantt: pilot city (M1) → cluster A deep-tech (M3–M5) → cluster C startup hub (M6–M8) → cluster B factories (M9–M14).
- Yellow bar: **M7 gate** = at least 10 labs and 90% on-time before the second ₹60L.
- New line: how we win customers (80% free channels, CAC ₹1,500, paid back in ~1 booking). **Funnel: A7.**

**Slide 7 · Results — the 18-month money chart**

- Three lines: upside (navy dashes), base (thick teal), downside (red dots). Base crosses zero at **Month 15** (yellow flag).
- Tiles: M15, 2,316 bookings, ₹38.6L cash used, ₹78.6L in downside.
- "What moves it most": booking value, capture, fixed cost.
- New red line: downside + Month-9 rescue → loss −₹2.32L, cash floor ₹47.7L. **Detail: A9.**
- RESULT: break-even needs two levers; without them −₹1.23L. Our +50% rush price matches a real lab's express fee [S9].

**Slide 8 · Results — the investor deal**

- Table of the investor's 6 conditions: ✓ hosts, ✓ on-time, ✓ break-even, ✓ capital; ✗ bookings (2,316 vs 2,400), ✗ repeat (57.8% vs 60%).
- Our stance: revise to 2,200 and 55%, and give a stricter quality bar in return.
- New "Why 55%" line: evidence says 51–55% is realistic. **Detail: A5.**

**Slide 9 · Method — the trust layer**

- Six steps: Slot → Sample → Test → Report → Pay → Resolve.
- Listing portal vs OpenBench Assured: who is accountable (nobody vs us).

**Slide 10 · Controls — checkpoints and KPIs**

**Slide 11 · Controls — risks and the ask**

- Ask: approve the Assured-first plan and the two-tranche offer. Last line: "Booked should mean delivered."
